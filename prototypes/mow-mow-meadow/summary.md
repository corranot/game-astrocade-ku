# Mow Mow Meadow: one-page summary

Status: plan waiting for the owner's approval (28 Nov 2026). Full plan: `plan.md`. Research:
`production/market/2026-11-28T0710-new-sim-dig/report.md`.

**What it is.** A portrait, 3D, fully code-built Three.js mowing simulator for kids (working title). Nilo, a
chibi child gardener, rides Buzzy, a friendly little mower with headlamp eyes, through wild gardens that belong
to animal neighbours. The grass starts taller than the mower; the player leaves perfect stripes, finds friends
and lost toys in the grass, and drives straight from one garden into the next.

**Why this concept.** One More Lawn is a PC game (Steam, 12 Nov 2026), not an Astrocade game; we take its genre
mechanics only (stripes that follow your driving, a live % cut, a client receipt, a 90% gate to the next job, a
sparkle on missed grass). On Astrocade today, coverage games lead the simulator and tidy-up rows (Canvas Flow
about 40K plays a day, a restoration simulator 23.5K a day on day one; proxy = gain between two row crawls,
27 to 28 Nov), but only the ones with a place to move through, a job chain, upgrades and a collection last and
reach Players' Choice. Eleven bare mowing simulators sit under 1,300 plays: nobody has built the genre properly
there. Scored against a leaf-vacuum park and a beach treasure tractor, mowing with animal clients won 68 to 47
and 51 of 70.

**The loop.**
1. Drag one thumb: Buzzy drives and cuts; every square metre plays a note of the tune and builds Flow.
2. Every 6 to 10 s the grass gives up a friend, a toy or coins; every 20 to 30 s a bale pops out to collect.
3. At 90% the gate swings open and the neighbour comes out to play (97% and 100% give more stars).
4. On the lane, drive onto a pad to upgrade Buzzy (wider deck, faster engine, bigger baler), then into the next
   garden, which has one new idea.

**How it aims at 10 minutes and more.** No start menu, no result screen, no "play again?": the next garden is
always in view. A reward at least every 6 s and something new at least every 25 s. Three open goals of
different lengths at all times (this stripe, this garden or upgrade, the district party or album page). Twelve
gardens in three districts take the model bot 22 minutes, then a Garden of the Day, three-star replays and
cosmetics. Honest note: no game in our data has a 10-minute median (our best is 119 s), so the targets are
staged: 90 s at the launch gate, 180 s after two tuning updates, 600 s as the stretch goal.

**What makes it look and feel special.** Grass as the star (one mask texture drives the blades, the stripes
and the %), a toy-garden look with enamel, felt and painted wood, three times of day, a tune the lawn itself
plays, layered effects on every interaction, cinematic beats on key moments, and hard comfort limits against
glare measured on every scene.

**Milestones.** M0 lawn spike (grass at 60 FPS, first frame in 2 s, go or no-go) → M1 the first minute (gardens
1 and 2, FTUE, Nilo and Buzzy, sound) → M2 District 1 (economy, upgrades, album, receipt, party) → M3 Districts
2 and 3 (patterns, dusk, daily garden, seven languages) → M4 polish and proof (full test plan, draft) → one
publish after the owner's thumbnail.

**Open choices (defaults in brackets).** Title (Mow Mow Meadow). Launch size (all three districts at once, or
District 1 first and the rest as day-3 and day-6 updates). Hero look (Nilo as described).
